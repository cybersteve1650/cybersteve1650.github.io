The starting point

Japan taxes high income heavily

The top national income tax rate is 45%. Resident tax adds a flat 10% on top. At the highest band you keep only 45 yen of every extra 100 yen you earn.

So the fastest way to build wealth here is not to earn more. It is to lower the income that gets taxed.

Lever 01 · Property investment

Build equity and lower your tax

A rental property in Japan usually runs at a paper loss in the early years, mostly because of depreciation. That loss comes off your salary income.

about ¥900,000 Taxable income removed per property, per year
Cash back in your pocket ¥450,000

What to buy

  • Units under 30 square metres
  • Tokyo and Osaka only
  • Areas with strong tenant demand
  • Within a 10 minute walk of a station

Typical price range is ¥18M to ¥40M. Small units let you own more properties and spread your risk across buildings. Many good deals in Tokyo never reach a public listing.

With permanent residence

100% loan

Banks lend the full purchase price. Cash needed up front is often under ¥1,000,000.

Without it

70% loan

You fund the remaining 30% yourself, and fewer banks will consider you.

What you can deduct

  • Depreciation and loan interest
  • Management fees, leasing fees and agent fees
  • Repairs, upkeep and insurance
  • Accountant and legal costs
  • Utilities you pay, and travel to the property
  • Loan and bank fees

Lever 02 · Angel fund investment

Back Japanese founders and claim the deduction

Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry runs an angel investor scheme. Money you put into qualifying early stage companies can be deducted from your income.

up to ¥8,000,000 Deduction available under the METI angel scheme
Cash back in your pocket ¥4,000,000

¥5,000,000

¥10,500,000

Why I chose the fund I use

  • The partners have built and sold their own companies
  • Careful attention to protecting shareholder capital
  • Access to the investor community and its events
  • Investors help review new deals and support the portfolio
  • Eight to twelve companies backed each year

Read the fees before you commit. Expect 2% to 3% each year, often paid up front for ten years. The tax deduction does not cover the fees.

This is a long hold. Treat it as money you will not see again for several years.

Lever 03 · Dependent deductions

Support family, lower your tax

If you send money to family who depend on you, in Japan or overseas, you can claim a deduction for each person.

¥380,000 General dependent. Age 16 to 18, or 23 to 69
You keep ¥190,000
¥480,000 Elderly dependent. Age 70 and above
You keep ¥240,000

The deduction is claimed per person, so several family members can qualify.

Four rules that decide if your claim is accepted

  1. Prove the relationshipA birth certificate translated into Japanese by an official translator. Around ¥3,000 plus postage, and about two days.
  2. Send the money inside one monthOne transfer window keeps all the evidence on a single bank statement.
  3. Keep proof from your sideStatements must show the money leaving your own account.
  4. Submit at year endGive the evidence to your employer during the year end tax adjustment.

Lever 04 · NISA

Tax free investing for life

Normally you pay 20.315% on investment gains and dividends in Japan. Inside a NISA account you pay nothing.

¥3.6MContribution limit each year
¥18MLifetime contribution limit

The yearly limit splits into ¥1.2M for stable funds and ¥2.4M for growth funds. Open an account with Rakuten, SBI or Monex. Set a monthly amount and leave it alone.

My own mix

Global stocks 50% · United States stocks 33% · India stocks 17%

United States taxpayers, take care. NISA only removes Japanese tax. You must still report and pay in the United States.

You can withdraw at any time, but check the rules first. Putting the money back in later has limits.

Lever 05 · Furusato nozei

Turn tax you already owe into goods you want

You donate to towns around Japan. Almost the whole donation comes back off your resident tax. In return the towns send you gifts. You pay ¥2,000 in total and keep the goods.

  1. Work out your limitUse an online calculator, after your other deductions are counted.
  2. Donate before 31 DecemberChoose the towns and pick your gifts.
  3. Receive the goodsWagyu, rice, fruit, furniture, appliances and shopping vouchers.
  4. Pay less resident taxYou see the reduction from June the following year.
¥564,000 Roughly what you can donate on a ¥20,000,000 salary

Gifts are capped at 30% of the donation value, so about ¥169,200 of goods each year.

Figures are donation amounts, not the retail price of the gift. Some wards also offer shopping vouchers usable at local stores.

Lever 06 · Permanent residence

The step that unlocks everything else

Permanent residence is not a tax lever by itself. It is the thing that makes the property lever work properly, so it is worth starting early.

100% property loans

Banks lend the full purchase price, so you need very little cash up front.

More banks will talk to you

A wider choice of lenders means better rates and better terms.

Security if you lose your job

Your right to stay in Japan no longer depends on your employer.

Simpler paperwork

No more visa renewals tied to your job title or your salary.

What the application looked like for me

  • Lawyer fee of about ¥160,000, quoted in 2025
  • Eighteen months from filing to approval
  • Handled in business English
  • One clear checklist of documents

Check your points score first. A higher score can shorten the residence period you need before you apply.
